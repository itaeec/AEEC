What Parents Can Expect During Their Child’s First Week of Prep

School prep13 October 20264 min read

Starting Prep is a major milestone for children and their families. After years of learning through play, attending childcare, and preparing for kindergarten, the first week of primary school brings exciting new experiences, friendships, and routines.

However, it’s also completely normal for children to feel tired, nervous, or overwhelmed as they adjust to their new environment. Understanding what to expect during the first week of

Prep can help parents support their child and make the transition to school a positive experience.

At Altona Early Education, we understand how important this milestone is. Preparing children for the move from kinder to primary school helps build the confidence and independence they need to approach this new chapter with enthusiasm.

What Happens During the First Week of Prep?

Every school has its own approach to welcoming new students, but the first week generally focuses on helping children become familiar with their classroom, teachers, classmates, and daily routines.

Your child may experience:

  • Meeting their teacher: Building a relationship with their teacher helps children feel safe and comfortable asking for help.
  • Learning classroom routines: Children begin practising where to put their bags, how to line up, and when to move between activities.
  • Making new friends: Group activities and playtime provide opportunities to meet classmates and develop friendships.
  • Exploring new learning activities: Children participate in stories, creative projects, early literacy, numeracy, and hands-on learning experiences.
  • Adjusting to a longer day: Depending on their previous routine, children may need time to adapt to the structure and demands of a school day.

It’s important to remember that every child settles in differently. Some will feel comfortable straight away, while others may need several weeks to adjust.

5 Ways to Help Your Child Settle Into Prep

1. Establish a Consistent Morning Routine

A predictable morning routine can help your child feel more prepared for school. Aim to allow enough time for breakfast, getting dressed, packing their bag, and travelling to school without unnecessary rushing. Preparing uniforms, lunch boxes, and school bags the night before can also make mornings less stressful.

2. Talk About Their Day

Give your child opportunities to share their experiences without making them feel pressured to answer lots of questions. Instead of simply asking, “How was school?”, try questions such as:

  • What was something fun you did today?
  • Did you learn anything new?
  • Who did you play with?
  • Was anything difficult today?

These conversations help you understand how your child is adjusting and give you opportunities to celebrate their achievements.

3. Expect Some Tiredness

Starting school can be mentally and physically demanding. Children are learning new routines, meeting unfamiliar people, and concentrating for extended periods. During the first few weeks, consider providing extra downtime after school, maintaining a consistent bedtime, and allowing time for relaxed family activities.

4. Encourage Independence

School requires children to manage more tasks independently than they may be used to. You can support this transition by encouraging your child to practise:

  • Opening their lunch box and drink bottle
  • Managing their school bag and belongings
  • Using the toilet independently
  • Asking their teacher for help
  • Following simple instructions

Celebrate their efforts rather than expecting them to master everything immediately.

5. Reassure Them About Their Feelings

Your child might feel excited one day and reluctant to attend school the next. These changing emotions can be a normal part of adjusting to a new environment. Listen to their concerns, acknowledge their feelings, and remind them that learning something new takes time. If difficulties persist, speak with their teacher about how you can work together to support them.

How Kinder Helps Prepare Children for School

A positive transition to Prep begins well before the first day of school. Kinder provides valuable opportunities for children to develop social skills, practise independence, follow routines, and build confidence through play-based learning.

At Altona Early Education, our kinder program supports children as they develop the foundations for their next educational milestone. Through meaningful learning experiences and supportive relationships with educators, children can gradually build the skills they need to approach school with greater confidence.

Preparing for Your Child’s Next Chapter

Starting Prep is an exciting step, and children benefit when families and educators work together to make the transition as smooth as possible.

If your child is approaching school age and you’d like to learn more about preparing them for this important milestone, contact Altona Early Education today. Our friendly team can answer your questions about our kinder program and how we support children as they prepare for their transition to primary school.
